# Independent peer review of the updated benchmark comparison

Reviewed 6 November 2026 UTC. Scope: [comparison report](<../../reports/TETHER benchmark comparison update 2026-11-06.md>) at SHA256 `5633e2a5103e747a2d7f2567b3a1697838b6ee897f740dc94aa2d6012aac1d33`, retained SciFact/LitQA2/Inspect results, and the existing source-qualified general/biomedical/implementation notes. **Judgment: the main recommendation is supported; three clarifications and one methodological refinement should precede reuse as a paper-level argument.** No aggregate-result discrepancy was found in this bounded review.

## Findings and suggested corrections

1. **Name both sides of the old timing ratio.** The report's “cold per-case 1.65 timing against TETHER” is ambiguous next to Inspect/TETHER = 1.5465. The old ratio is **TETHER / independent validator ≈ 1.65**, measured in fresh processes per case. The new median paired batch ratio TETHER / independent is 0.9443. Replace the ambiguous clause in this report and the analogous sentence in the [Inspect results](<../../planning/TETHER Inspect Comparison Results 2026-11-06.md>). Neither execution condition contradicts the other, and neither is an architecture-wide speed claim.

2. **Qualify annotation availability by ScholarQABench subset.** The official repository lists released rubric labels for ScholarQA-CS and reference-based evaluation for ScholarQA-Multi; its Bio and Neuro rows have no correctness-label entry and list citation evaluation. Therefore using existing annotations can avoid a new expert-recruitment gate for an appropriate subset, but cannot silently supply answer-correctness labels for every biomedical subset. Add this distinction to the benchmark table/final recommendation. The repository's binary ScholarQA-SciFact derivative is also not a replacement for the original document/rationale SciFact task executed here. [Official task/annotation table](https://github.com/AkariAsai/ScholarQABench#repository-organizations).

3. **Separate a stronger released solver from a controlled architecture ablation.** The retained linear baseline used 380 fit claims after source-component exclusions. MultiVerS' official recipe uses FEVER plus weakly labelled PubMedQA/Evidence Inference before target fine-tuning; its released SciFact checkpoint additionally uses a specified negative-sampling regime. A pretrained checkpoint is a legitimate external solver baseline, but any gain would combine architecture and supervision differences. For an architecture claim, match task supervision, allowed source exposure and tuning rules; otherwise label the condition explicitly and do not attribute its gain to the harness. Pin the exact checkpoint variant rather than treating every “MultiVerS” model as interchangeable. [Official training recipe](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/dwadden/multivers/main/doc/training.md), [retained fit and split](<../../planning/TETHER SciFact Native Evaluation Results 2026-11-05.md>).

4. **Describe the SciFact bottleneck as post-retrieval selection/label/rationale error, pending decomposition.** The 147/188 full-document-set retrieval result makes a stronger joint model a reasonable hypothesis, but strict case failure also reflects extra predicted documents and rationale completeness. It does not isolate faulty biological interpretation. The report already gives this caveat earlier; carry it into the final recommendation. Before or alongside a new model, decompose the sealed baseline's document false positives, label mistakes and rationale failures descriptively. Any new threshold or selection rule belongs in a separately frozen experiment with fit/tune-only selection, not a retrospective development-set repair. “Improvement is the scientific success criterion; a negative result still completes the measurement” is clearer than the potentially ambiguous “require improved useful correct answers.”

## Statements that are appropriately supported

- SciFact's 27/300 strict correct answers, 159/209 annotated document-pair retrieval, 147/188 complete-set retrieval and identical raw/checked official metrics agree with the retained reconstruction. The 112/300 constant-NEI reference is correctly labelled post hoc and not an executed arm.
- LitQA2's 12 correct, 21 substantive and 178 unsure responses agree with the native reconstruction. The 10/205 DOI-pair and 10/199 question-hit diagnostics agree with the independent source audit. The report correctly avoids treating DOI identity as entailment, no DOI hit as definitive source absence, or the 6/10 versus 6/189 comparison as causal evidence.
- Inspect's 981 cells are repeated finite conformance constructions, not scientific samples. Reused policy code, common supervisor, incomplete runtime port and different cost conditions are disclosed. Generic logging, limits and orchestration are not claimed as new.
- PaperQA2 supports testing query-conditioned summarization/reranking and citation traversal, but also reports accuracy degradation with some summarization models. The proposed raw-passage control is therefore justified; no published gain is transferred to TETHER. [Original methods and ablations](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.13740v1).
- ALCE, R2E and ScholarQABench answer different questions; none validates clinical success. The report correctly separates native uncertainty, policy rejection and operational failure, and treats public data/exposed development results as such. Virtual Biotech/Inspect overlap and the absence of a qualified full competitor execution appropriately narrow novelty claims.

These corrections do not require another completed-stage run. They sharpen the next experiment's hypotheses and the limits of the comparative claim; BG06 closure remains distinct from future semantic-model work.
