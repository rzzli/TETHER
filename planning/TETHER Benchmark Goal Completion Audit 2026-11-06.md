# TETHER benchmark execution: final goal audit

6 November 2026 UTC. This audits the owner's five-stage [execution plan](<TETHER Benchmark Execution Plan 2026-11-02.md>) and consolidates the current [peer-method review](<../reports/TETHER benchmark comparison update 2026-11-06.md>). The five implementation/evaluation stages are complete in their declared scopes. The final independent requirements, preservation and scientific-scope reviews are retained with this report. This is completion of the bounded benchmark goal, not completion of the clinical research programme, a full Inspect migration or submission readiness.

The most consequential result is negative: the native scientific-task checks preserved traceability but did not improve answer correctness. SciFact has 27/300 strict-correct predictions; LitQA2 has 12/199 correct answers and 178 explicit unsure responses. The policy controls admitted every candidate in both studies. A real Inspect extension achieves the same finite archive conformance, but this experiment does not demonstrate a simpler or faster complete replacement for TETHER.

The BG03/BG04 task adapters enforce their declared native source contracts; they do not inherit every therapeutic clause in BG02's27-obligation policy. BG05 archive and runtime coverage are separately reported, as required by its protocol. The plan explicitly permits reporting portability gaps; it does not permit calling an incomplete port fully equivalent.

The raw and checked scientific arms share the same generated candidates and source pipeline. They are not separately timed, independent full end-to-end systems. For fixed candidates, admission filtering cannot increase correct answers per all scheduled cases; it can change admitted error, selective precision and coverage. Both studies admit100%, so they demonstrate neither such a tradeoff nor an accuracy improvement. A new retrieval/repair/generation experiment is needed to test useful-answer improvement.

## Data, chronology and evaluation boundaries

Sources, models, packages, reference roles and code are pinned in the respective stage records. Historical AACT/clinical assets are not new scientific benchmarks in this sequence. No clinical outcome, MR estimate, drug-target probability or causal-genetics validation is inferred from these results.

- **Reference isolation:** SciFact labels/rationales and LitQA2 answers/source hints remain evaluator-only during generation. Inspect's oracle is joined after candidate sealing. These are trusted-workflow controls, not OS-level malicious-agent isolation.
- **Exposure and independence:** SciFact public development, original public LitQA2 and authored policy controls are exposed. SciFact fit/tune exclusion rules do not make model pretraining or the public corpus globally unseen. LitQA2's186 source/question components are not199 independent studies. No retrospective renaming creates a fresh holdout.
- **Rights and missingness:** LitQA2 retrieval used the declared CC-BY/CC0 route; uncollected annotated sources were not filled from answer hints. Full-text access, source identity and answer support are distinct. Public benchmark/code licenses do not automatically grant all incorporated article/model rights. The retained per-stage source qualifications remain authoritative.
- **Scientific labels:** Exact quotes, immutable records and valid source hashes do not prove entailment, appropriate statistics, absence of bias or clinical truth. Independent reconstruction here means separately implemented AI-assisted engineering review in the same project, not external human scientific adjudication.
- **Accounting:** Every scheduled case remains represented, including wrong/unsure, malformed, failed, timed-out, interrupted and unexecuted outcomes where present. A correctly rejected fault can score as policy conformance without being a completed scientific answer.

## Resources and implementation efficiency

The evidence does not support a single system-wide speed ratio. BG02 uses fresh processes per case; BG05 uses fresh serial batches with warm within-batch imports. TETHER/independent is1.65 in the former and0.9443 in the latter. Different wrappers/instrumentation/granularity prevent isolating one cause. Inspect/TETHER is1.5465 in the matched BG05 batch condition, with three descriptive repetitions and no claim of statistical significance.

Repeated proof checks dominate the small SciFact workflow:486.680 of587.287 reported seconds are runtime/source brackets. Those checks protect shared fitting, tuning, generation, scoring and checking, so assigning all their time to marginal answer checking would be wrong. The measured checking-branch subtotal is28.795 seconds; other residual work is unallocated rather than invented.

LitQA2's original controller has a fixed-large-read allocation issue and finalization outside its worker deadline. Those frozen limitations are retained, not repaired retroactively to improve recorded timing. Its882.172-second evaluation includes extensive source/model verification; that is not isolated admission latency. A smaller native-score-only reader does fewer obligations. BG05 uses actual-size bounded reads and reduces repeated third-party hashing while keeping complete global and narrower per-batch proofs. No scientific-answer improvement follows from this engineering optimization.

Primary Inspect outer wall time is410.430 seconds; runtime witness outer time77.251 seconds. Runtime parent proof/finalization overhead is measured but not hard-preempted; process-tree CPU and whole-stage peak memory remain unavailable. The source inventory distinguishes new policy, reused validator, common orchestration, tests, analysis and upstream packages. Its freeze-based scope excludes later closure helpers. Neither total package lines nor a small routing wrapper measures equivalent maintenance effort.

Provider spending remains **$0**. Local compute/platform cost was not priced. Scripted runtime token counts are fixture telemetry; SciFact's non-LLM counts are not applicable; LitQA2's actual local model/tokenizer work is separately reported. These quantities must not be pooled as paid-provider usage.

## Peer comparison and next scientific decision

The [updated comparison](<../reports/TETHER benchmark comparison update 2026-11-06.md>) and [independent review](https://github.com/rzzli/TETHER/blob/tether-pre-namespace-migration-20261106/research_notes/TETHER%20benchmark%20priorities%20and%20methods/PEER-REVIEW-20261106-03.md) identify the useful next methods:

1. Decompose sealed SciFact errors into document selection, label and rationale failures. Then test a qualified full-context joint solver, using MultiVerS as a concrete methodological peer. An externally trained checkpoint changes supervision/exposure; it is not a matched architecture ablation unless those factors are controlled.
2. For LitQA2, test bounded query expansion and reranking, inspired by PaperQA2. Keep original spans beside any generated summaries; summaries may remove important qualifications. A supplied-source diagnostic must be labelled separately from end-to-end retrieval.
3. Evaluate one bounded answer-repair/retrieval-refinement step, following the relevant OpenScholar idea, against one-pass and critique-only controls at matched total budgets. Count newly damaged correct answers and additional cost as well as repaired errors.
4. Test evidence dependence through removal, rationale-only and irrelevant/conflicting-context interventions. ERASER-style measurements require their native assumptions; existing evidence masks alone do not establish a scientific faithfulness result.
5. Use ALCE for citation support, then a suitable ScholarQABench subset for synthesis and R2E for target-side ranking. ScholarQABench Bio/Neuro lack released correctness labels in the inspected guide; its CS/Multi annotations cannot silently validate all biomedical answers.

Self-RAG's learned retrieval/critique signals are another method to investigate; a reflection prompt is not an implementation of that trained method. Virtual Biotech's current manifests/provenance and Inspect's general limits/logs overlap with TETHER, narrowing novelty claims. Biomni's executable biomedical analysis is a later task-specific extension, not a prerequisite to this bounded goal. No full Virtual Biotech/Biomni reproduction or peer headline-performance comparison was executed.

These are recommendations, not newly started model runs, downloads or goal requirements. A negative result remains a completed measurement. No new external expert panel is requested; released annotations can support only the endpoints/subsets they actually cover.
