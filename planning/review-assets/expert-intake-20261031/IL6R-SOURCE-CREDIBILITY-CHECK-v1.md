The inference of a **source-level credibility problem** is stronger than merely saying that primary methods are unavailable. It does not establish replication quality, clinical efficacy, an intervention recommendation or a complete appraisal of either primary study. The primary abstracts were checked; their entire methods/results were not independently reproduced or fully audited. In particular, Rochas's healthy-donor B-cell attribution is newly checked external context, not a field supplied in P5; do not impute it into the original card or ratings. No formal erratum or author intent is established.

Source fidelity and scientific credibility therefore need separate fields. A candidate may quote a questionable source faithfully. Conversely, a claim may be supported elsewhere but mis-cited to a passage that does not entail it. A missing-detail judgment about the supplied cards is not overturned merely because additional detail is found outside those cards. These distinctions are compatible and should not be collapsed into a single exam score or majority vote.
