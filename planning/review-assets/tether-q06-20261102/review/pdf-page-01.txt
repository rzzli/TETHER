Abstract
Biomedical agent reports can preserve a citation while changing a number, importing later information or
concealing an unavailable field. We present TETHER, a versioned evidence-workflow implementation, and a
bounded technical study of literal source reporting. The study tests source fidelity and admission policy, rather
than therapeutic judgment. Before execution, we fixed twelve units selected by identity from archived Open
Targets 25.09 data, source-only generation inputs, separately reconstructed references, an all-output schedule
and a descriptive analysis. Each unit requests eleven identity fields and four native measurement fields; forty of
forty-eight measurement slots are populated. A deterministic literal assembler produces verified exact reports
in twelve of twelve units and completes every answerable measurement group at the frozen 60%, 80% and
100% thresholds. The 80% and 100% criteria are mathematically identical for all twelve native units, so they
are not separate robustness evidence. An always-abstain control completes zero of twelve. In fourteen
exposed constructed scenarios, the full policy admits none of ten deliberately invalid reports and rejects none
of four technically valid reports. Removing the study-specific eligibility wrapper admits two invalid scenarios;
retaining only byte anchoring and output-schema checks admits seven. All thirty-eight outputs and ninety policy
decisions are retained. Production takes 203.828 seconds, of which 97.0485% is source verification, followed
by a separate 7.125-second source-inclusive audit. Earlier local generator probes failed their declared
contracts and remain negative development evidence; no audit-stage model call occurred. The separately
qualified post-study TETHER 0.5.0 release includes scoped verification, child supervision and artifact-specific
admission; those guarantees are not attributed to the frozen experiment. The evidence supports
reconstructable reporting on this selected set, not clinical accuracy, causal genetic interpretation,
independent-study generalization, model superiority or provider authenticity. Numerical prediction features
remain empty and clinical probabilities null.
1. Introduction
Therapeutic assessment combines sources with different release dates, identifiers, biological contexts and
permissions. A report can be faithful to its retained bytes yet use an ineligible source or convey an unsupported
conclusion. A missing measurement is also different from an absent biological effect. An auditable workflow
therefore needs to distinguish byte integrity, mechanical consistency and scientific validity.

therapeutic-development functions [3]. Existing approaches also evaluate evidence support. Alvessa checks
