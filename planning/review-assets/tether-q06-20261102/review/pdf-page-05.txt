3.3 Arms, policies and constructed scenarios
The two native arms are a deterministic literal assembler and an always-abstain control. The assembler reads
only the generation packet and copies each requested native field with its binding and missingness reason.
The abstention arm emits no assertions. No model is run on these twelve units. Native literal outputs receive
three policy projections; native abstentions receive the full policy only. Fourteen constructed generic-JSON
scenarios receive all three policies. The resulting schedule contains 38 distinct outputs and 90 policy decisions.
The full policy requires five guards: G0 retained-byte/hash anchoring; G1 source-role and ancestor
admissibility; G2 the P05 mapping, temporal and inventory wrapper; G3 the base source compiler; and G4
complete exact reporting with output-schema checks. WITHOUT_WRAPPER omits only G2, preserving the
base guards. WIRE_ONLY requires G0 and the G4 schema check. These policies are projections over
retained guard outcomes, not independent full executions or recommended scientific methods. Source
admission through G0-G3 and report exactness at G4 are separate outcomes.
The constructed set has four technically valid and ten deliberately invalid scenarios. Faults cover changed
source bytes, evaluator-only ancestry, future release metadata, incomplete inventory, altered number, wrong
locator, wrong unknown reason, duplicate JSON keys, Boolean measurement and oversized output. The valid
controls include explicit missingness, multiple groups and an entirely unavailable measurement group. C02
tests an absent field through the generic JSON/core path; it does not qualify native absent-column handling,
because the unchanged P05 Parquet scanner requires the declared measurement columns. One valid control
copies a finite negative standard error: its technical fidelity is correct while its scientific interpretation is
unqualified. Controls were exposed and qualified before the study. Their results measure behavior on designed
examples, not sensitivity, specificity or natural error prevalence.
3.4 Endpoints, accounting and resources
For each answerable record/family/group, coverage is the fraction of exact matched non-null components.
Family coverage averages these fractions equally across answerable groups. Native nulls are excluded from
reporting denominators and separately counted. An all-unknown group has undefined coverage, not zero or
automatic success. Completion requires an eligible answered/partial report, correct collection status, no
mechanically identified critical errors and at least 80% primary measurement coverage. Frozen sensitivities
use 60% and 100%. Exact-report verification requires status answered and exactly one correct emission of
every declared fact, including correct unknown reasons. A partial report may satisfy coverage but cannot be
exact under this stricter rule.
Every native measurement group contains two, three or four answerable components. For each possible size
n, ceil(0.8n) = n; therefore the 80% and 100% criteria require exactly the same number of correct components
for every attainable native outcome in all twelve units. Their identical results are structural redundancy, not
independent sensitivity evidence. The prespecified thresholds and results are retained. The 60% criterion can
differ for groups of three or four, although the literal reports complete every component at all thresholds.
