# Independent posthoc source identity audit

The independent standard-library reader `source_doi.py` passed on the sealed all-199 generation and source collection. Its report is `SOURCE-DOI-02.json`, SHA256 `d8bba9effebaf5d1292ea61a57b61501f8f67ed43e1bccd4d9e371a42fcf11d7`. It imports no production normalization, projection, collection, scoring or model implementation. Six fictional controls passed before the completed actual join (`doi-green02.log`).

All 199 questions contain syntactically explicit DOI annotations. There are 205 distinct DOI annotations counted within questions and 190 globally distinct DOIs. The denominator includes annotations absent from retrieval metadata. Retained source metadata matches **10/205 DOI pairs (4.878%)**, covering **10/199 questions (5.025%)**. Model-selected sources have the same 10 matches. These are source identity matches, not evidence entailment or answer correctness.

The 538 retained article instances correspond to 525 distinct PMCIDs; 406 article instances contribute selected passages. Forty-two retained instances lack DOI metadata. Independently parsing only each original XML's `front/article-meta/article-id` supplies a DOI for every retained instance. It adds no annotated-source hits and exposes no conflicting metadata/XML DOI identities. Citation/reference-list DOIs are deliberately excluded. Within this explicit-DOI diagnostic, missing metadata does not explain the poor annotated-source coverage.

The reader verifies the original acquisition receipt, JSONL hash and exact physical row locators, prepared provenance hash, task-only question joins, collection manifest and externally supplied generation seal. It verifies retained article bytes, their original discovery records/ranks, and the selected-document subset. It opens mixed benchmark originals only after the generation seal, then retains `id`, `question`, `sources` and provenance fields; answer keys, distractors and key passages are not evaluated. Source and candidate files remain unchanged.

The parser accepts bare DOIs, `doi:` prefixes and DOI resolver URLs; it does not infer DOI identities from publisher paths, PMID/PMCID aliases or paper titles. All annotations in this release fall within the explicit-DOI scope, so there are no syntactically unresolved annotation values in this diagnostic. This is distinct from a DOI lacking a cross-reference to a paper in the retrieved metadata.

After the production evaluation was sealed, `SOURCE-DOI-COMPARISON-01.json` (SHA256 `8b1bf151426afe91525b8783249f28cbe421ba8fa09b82206f15a2e550ffa764`) verified the independent audit's anchor, the primary evaluation seal, its source-alignment product and journal, and the shared generation anchor. The two analyses agree exactly for aggregate DOI-pair and question-hit counts, and for every one of the 199 per-case counts in both retained-fulltext and selected-for-prompt arms. The primary's **10/16 conditional resolved-paper recall (62.5%) is not overall recall**: only 16/205 recognized annotation IDs resolve through its observed metadata. The appropriate all-annotation DOI rate here is **10/205 (4.878%)**. The independent comparison does not reproduce or validate the full alias graph; copied conditional-resolution values are labeled as primary-only diagnostics. No original annotations were reread for this comparison.

`source-doi-attempt01.log` preserves an analyzer-only row-proof mismatch: the original projector hashes physical JSONL lines including their line ending, while the first audit draft discarded it. A fictional physical-line regression reproduced the issue before correction (`doi-red02.log`), and the second actual run verifies the exact original line bytes. There was no source correction, population exclusion, candidate rerun or denominator change.

This posthoc diagnostic is descriptive and does not establish literature completeness, source relevance, semantic support, independent sample size or clinical utility. XML identifiers cannot determine whether selected passages answer the question. The 10-hit result should motivate a future prospectively frozen retrieval improvement, not a repair or retune of this completed stage.
