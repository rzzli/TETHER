# Results and current scope

**Updated 13 November 2026.** TETHER has working evidence/accounting machinery,
contemporary source-reporting examples and completed development measurements.
It has **no qualified historical clinical forecaster, validated clinical
probabilities or held-out demonstration of scientific agent superiority**.

This register reconciles retained artifacts; it is not a new experiment or a
fresh replay of all original datasets. Hashes below identify the exact records.
Raw datasets, weights, private run bundles and machine-specific manifests are
not distributed here. A clean clone runs the fictional demo; full research
reproduction requires additional permitted inputs and original environments.
See [dependencies](FRAMEWORK.md#supported-and-study-specific-paths) and
[verification limits](TETHER-VERIFICATION-SCOPE.md).

## Temporal data feasibility

Five complete OT25.09 evidence partitions passed source integrity and aggregate
date profiling, covering 500 files and 2,609,207 rows. The two ChEMBL sizing
passes used all 573,103 rows in 100 files and yielded 74,187 distinct stored
target–disease pairs. The second pass added annual horizon counts after the
first aggregate results were viewed. Its existing global counts reproduced.

Under the frozen descriptive rules, 6,649 pairs have reported phase 2 entry in
2016–2019. Among 5,746 calendar-mature pairs without a declared source-history
hold, 231 have a phase 3 candidate within 36 months and 412 within 60 months.
The supplied review reported 344 and 544. Its incomplete at-risk definition and
unavailable implementation prevent exact reconciliation. Registry-reported
actual initiation, prior approvals, complete follow-up and labels still require
qualification. The source-observed counts yield zero qualified clinical labels.

An outcome-independent provisional designation groups the same 6,649 pairs
into 683 components based on possible earliest-entry drug and trial links.
Development contains 6,550 pairs. The exploratory reserve contains 99 pairs
in 49 components with incomplete exposure information. The largest component
has 2,278 pairs. Nominal pair counts therefore provide no independent-sample or
power estimate.

An archive comparison across 1,000 gene-burden and UniProt files compared
literal provider IDs, target–disease pairs and stored fields. At the January
2024 cutoff the modern gene-burden view filtered by `evidenceDate` shared 3,994
pairs with the selected 22.11 archive, with 1,467 pairs unique to the archive
and 3,489 unique to the filtered modern view. The analogous UniProt counts
were 2,953 shared, 1,070 archive-only and 3,617 modern-only. The January 2025
comparison used the selected 24.09 archives. The archive snapshots precede
their cutoffs. The filtered modern records carry modern mappings, and the
literal differences do not establish historical biological availability.

The 500-file Expression Atlas comparison covered 698,310 rows. Both modern
date fields selected the same 193,083 rows at both cutoffs. The January 2024
comparison yielded 127,626 shared pairs, 33,813 archive-only pairs and 10,856
filtered-modern-only pairs. In January 2025 the counts were 133,039, 27,588
and 5,443. Across the full 24.09 and 25.09 releases, 226,352 provider IDs
matched. Stored log2 fold change differed on 294 of those IDs and percentile
rank differed on 144. The comparisons measure release sensitivity under
literal keys. They leave the reasons for individual differences unresolved.

The complete OT25.09 ChEMBL scan selected 20,831 records for the sealed 6,550
development pairs. Independent reduction reproduced every unit disposition.
Original-row replay checked all 338 rows supporting the 100 prespecified
sampled trails. The other 20,493 selected records were replayed at the
projection level. Later trial or drug links triggered a prespecified reserve
quarantine for 2,336 development pairs in 11 linked components.

The finite-source candidate fraction varies from 2.22% to 44.27% at 36 months
and from 4.01% to 46.06% at 60 months under the two extreme classifications
of unresolved units, after 185 origin exclusions. These are classification
sensitivity ranges for this development frame. Clinical labels and an
independent test cohort remain unqualified.

Grouping the same development projections by exact drug, target and disease
yielded 7,720 triples with a reported phase-2 entry in 2016–2019. With a
separately derived origin per triple, 166 and 283 met the finite-source

triples remained unresolved. Literal drug identity leaves programme
equivalence open.

An exploratory diagnostic using the OT25.09 disease hierarchy found 51
2016-entry infertility pairs linked to the same candidate trial and drug.
All 51 were excluded or unresolved under the frozen audit rules. Among the
remaining audit candidates across all indications, one trial links 51 of
141 pairs at 36 months and 51 of 255 pairs at 60 months. These counts show
dependence among the recorded candidates. The diagnostic uses contemporary
disease categories.

L1 and L2 use the **same 199 repeatedly exposed public questions**, grouped into
186 publication components. The answer producer is local Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct

| Expanded retrieval, model ordering (E/R) | 15 | 14 | 23 |

No final-answer production/parse failures occurred in these five conditions.
Intermediate expansion had six codec failures with lexical fallback; ordering
fell back on 105/196 applicable L/R cases and 91/164 E/R cases. These measure the
declared fallback policies, not a reranker that worked on every question.
All 12 primary publication-component intervals include or touch zero. E/R minus
L/O raw accuracy is +4.02 percentage points, 95% interval [0, 8.21]. Intervals use
10,000 paired component bootstrap draws, seed 20261106, and are exploratory,
not multiplicity-controlled evidence of benefit.

Supplied papers have privileged identities, different availability and collection
budgets. Their higher point estimate is not a matched-budget effect or an oracle
ceiling. Older 12/199 LitQA results used another protocol and are not this
seven-correct L/O reference.

Repair damages four of seven formerly correct cases and recovers one formerly
unsure case. Its 10,000-draw paired publication-component intervals are pointwise

## Exposure, gaps and current work

The result-informed sequence is explicit: SciFact baseline → diagnosis → different
supervised solver → calibration; LitQA baseline → retrieval/ordering → supplied
sources → critique/repair → revised method after qualification failure. These
are development sets. A later freeze cannot erase exposure.

A check that retains or suppresses an answer cannot increase total correct yield.
It can improve conditional precision while reducing coverage. Existing
contemporary-cutoff checks therefore **do not measure temporal-admission benefit**.
A suitable experiment must vary eligible information before inference.

ALCE native controls exist, but coordinator qualification failed during listener
ownership inspection after starting/stopping its server. All five generation
slots remained unexecuted; model-load completion is unknown. Full benchmark
generations and scores remain zero. ALCE is frozen incomplete; Scholar/R2E are
deferred. Historical clinical training and locked clinical evaluation remain
unqualified.

Computed derivation and fixed-horizon evaluation passed their scoped
engineering checks. Current work qualifies source history, endpoint
ascertainment and the published baseline before model evaluation. Owner-supplied
paper declarations remain pending.
